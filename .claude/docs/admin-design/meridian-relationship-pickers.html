Five Relationships, Five Pickers

A recipe credits its cooks, files itself under a cuisine and a course, lists its ingredients, and suggests what to serve with it. Each of those is a relationship, and its settings choose the picker it gets in the drawer. The drawer below works: add an ingredient that doesn't exist yet, reorder the cooks with ⌥↑ ⌥↓ or by dragging, search Courses for something nested, and fill Pairs With to its limit.

Pasta alla Norma

/recipes/pasta-alla-norma
Fried aubergine, a quick tomato sauce, and ricotta salata grated over the top while the pasta is still hot.

## Ingredients

- 2 medium aubergines, in 2 cm cubes
- 400 g rigatoni
- 60 g ricotta salata

## Method

Salt the aubergine and leave it in a colander for 30 minutes, so it fries without drinking the oil.

Hundreds or Thousands of Candidates

A site with 1,240 recipes, 600 ingredients and 300 profiles is ordinary. So no picker ever renders the whole set past a small threshold, and no result list scrolls: each one is capped, ranked, and says how much more there is.

  • The threshold is the API's count, not a guess. The type reports how many candidates a relationship has; under 50 the picker loads them once with the entry, over 50 it never asks for the whole set.
  • Search runs from the first character, 200ms after typing stops. A response for an older query is dropped, so results never flash back to something already typed past.
  • A capped list ends in one line, not a scrollbar: “8 of 214. Keep typing to narrow.” If the API can't count cheaply, it says “More match. Keep typing.” instead of inventing a number.
  • Chosen values load by slug with the entry, in one request, so a recipe with 40 ingredients opens complete without running 40 searches.
  • Tokens fold past 12 chips into “+28 more”, which expands in place. The field stays one glance tall.
  • Nothing here is virtualized, because nothing is long enough to need it. The cap is the performance strategy.
