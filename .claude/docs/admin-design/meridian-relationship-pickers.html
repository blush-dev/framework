Five Relationships, Five Pickers

A recipe credits its cooks, files itself under a cuisine and a course, lists its ingredients, and suggests what to serve with it. Each of those is a relationship, and its settings choose the picker it gets in the drawer. The drawer below works: add an ingredient that doesn't exist yet, reorder the cooks with ⌥↑ ⌥↓ or by dragging, search Courses for something nested, fill Pairs With to its limit, and open Linked From at the foot of the drawer.

  • The threshold is the API's count, not a guess. The type reports how many candidates a relationship has; under 50 the picker loads them once with the entry, over 50 it never asks for the whole set.
  • Search runs from the first character, 200ms after typing stops. A response for an older query is dropped, so results never flash back to something already typed past.

  • Tokens fold past 12 chips into “+28 more”, which expands in place. The field stays one glance tall.
  • Nothing here is virtualized, because nothing is long enough to need it. The cap is the performance strategy.

The foot of the entry tab

Read-Only Panels

What points at this entry, and what this entry is for when it introduces an archive, are facts the author can't change here. They live in other entries' files, or in the type. So they aren't fields: they are rows pinned to the end of the tab, after every field and above Outline. They scroll with the fields rather than staying stuck to the drawer. On a short tab they sit at the drawer's bottom edge; on a long one they follow the last field. Each row says enough to answer the quick question, and opens a level down, the way Outline does, for the full answer.

  • Pinned, not sticky. The rows are always the last thing in the tab, whatever the type's fields are, in a fixed order: Archive Page, Linked From, Outline. They scroll with the fields, and nothing is ever added below Outline.
  • Up to three linkers are named in the row; more are counted by relationship. Either way the row is one line, so the end of the tab never grows with the data.
  • Opening one replaces the tab's fields and the rows under them, as Outline already does. The path back says where you came from; Escape goes back too.
  • Live linkers and drafts only. A draft is listed and marked, because it will link here when it is published. The trash is never listed.
  • A group shows 8. Up to 50, Show More expands it in place; over 50, the rest is a link to the linking type's list, filtered to this entry. The drill-down never becomes a list screen.
  • Linked From with nothing is a quiet row, not a gap. On a term or a profile, “Nothing links here” is the answer someone opened the drawer for.
When an entry stops being live

Leaving the Site

Moving a recipe to draft or to the trash quietly takes it off every live page that links to it. When that would happen, the action stops once to say how many live entries link to it and which, and names its button for the outcome. When nothing live links, it stays the one-click action it is now. A save refused by a limit is the one case that stops the author in the editor, because there the save did not happen.

6 · Saving the form over existing entries

Changing or Removing a Relationship

Five outcomes, five shapes. A change that would break entries is refused, and lists them. A change that would rewrite files asks, with the rewrite as a checkbox whose default is the safe choice. A change that only tightens the rules warns, with counts. Lists stop at 8, as everywhere else.
